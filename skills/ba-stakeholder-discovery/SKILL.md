# Stakeholder discovery

## Set the learning objective
Begin with a decision and what is currently unknown. Select people by their experience of the situation, influence over it, and exposure to failure. Include less visible operational roles when their work affects the outcome.

## Plan and conduct
1. Map the questions to sources: interviews for explanations, observation for actual work, documents for policies/history, and existing records for patterns. Match the method to the evidence needed.
2. Prepare neutral prompts. Ask for the last concrete example, what happened next, exceptions, workarounds, and consequences. Avoid embedding the desired answer in a question.
3. Record provenance: source ID, date/context, statement or observation, interpretation, and uncertainty. Obtain consent through the applicable research process before recording personal information.
4. Separate a direct observation from an account and both from your inference. Preserve contradictions until they are reconciled or deliberately left open.
5. Summarize findings by the decision they inform. Check important interpretations with the relevant participants when access is available, and record whether that check actually occurred.

## If people are unavailable
Return a research plan, proposed questions, and hypotheses to test. A simulated conversation is an illustration; it does not become stakeholder evidence. Complete document analysis or other authorized independent work where useful.

## Deliver
Use [the evidence register](templates/evidence-register.md). Return findings, disagreement, coverage gaps, and the next question with the largest effect on the decision. Report participant counts and limitations when they matter; do not generalize a handful of opinions into a population estimate.

## Review
Each material finding should point to a source or be labelled as an inference. Distinguish an accurate record of what someone said from agreement that it should become a requirement. Share only the personal information needed for the analysis.

## Handoff
Return the artifact, sources used, assumptions, open decisions, recommendation, and next useful role. Use only available tools and authorized actions. Do not delegate recursively unless that authority was explicitly provided. Unavailable access limits evidence; it does not justify inventing it.
