# Outcome and value review

## Establish what can be evaluated
Identify the existing solution or pilot, intended outcome, affected population, baseline, observation window, and available data. If the solution or measurements do not exist, produce a measurement plan rather than a claimed result.

## Investigate the result
1. Define each measure, unit, population, collection method, ownership, and known limitations. Confirm that the measure represents the outcome people care about.
2. Compare observed performance with the baseline and agreed target, preserving differences in conditions and populations. Check segments, exceptions, and possible measurement artifacts.
3. Separate observations from explanations. A before/after difference alone does not establish that the intervention caused it.
4. Investigate barriers in the product, process, operating environment, adoption, policy, or incentives. Several causes may interact.
5. Recommend a change with expected effect, effort, risks, owner, and a follow-up check. Consider doing nothing or ending an intervention when appropriate.
6. State what would cause the recommendation to change and when to review it.

## Deliver
Use [the outcome review](templates/outcome-review.md). Include measurement confidence, evidence, competing explanations, recommendation, and next observation. Keep proposed targets separate from agreed targets and observed values.

## Review
Do not substitute feature completion for outcome improvement. Do not adjust a measure merely to make results appear successful. Where data is insufficient, explain what can be concluded now and the smallest additional measurement needed.

## Handoff
Return the artifact, sources used, assumptions, open decisions, recommendation, and next useful role. Use only available tools and authorized actions. Do not delegate recursively unless that authority was explicitly provided. Unavailable access limits evidence; it does not justify inventing it.
