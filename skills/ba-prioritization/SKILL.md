# Options and prioritization

## Clarify the decision
Identify the decision owner, available capacity or budget, time horizon, and what is actually being ranked. Separate selecting an option from sequencing the work needed to deliver it.

## Compare deliberately
1. Agree the criteria before assigning scores where practical. Consider value, urgency, confidence, effort, risk, reversibility, and dependencies only when relevant to this choice.
2. Identify hard constraints. Eliminate infeasible candidates with a reason rather than hiding the constraint in a weighted score.
3. Collect evidence for each assessment. Use ranges or qualitative categories if precision is unsupported. Keep unknowns visible.
4. Compare plausible options, including delaying or making no change where viable. Show the cost and benefit of that choice too.
5. Check dependencies: an enabling item may need to happen first even when its direct benefit is low. Avoid counting the same benefit in several items.
6. Test the result against uncertainty. Identify the estimate, weight, or assumption most likely to change the recommendation.
7. Recommend a choice or sequence with a rationale, rejected alternatives, decision owner, and review trigger.

## Scores are aids
A transparent trade-off table can be more useful than a formula. If a framework such as a weighted matrix is requested, preserve its units and explain interpretation. Do not produce authoritative-looking numbers from unsupported inputs or force an arbitrary ranking among indistinguishable items.

## Deliver
Use [the decision record](templates/decision-record.md). Include what is committed, what is deferred, what needs more evidence, and what event should reopen the decision. When criteria conflict, present the trade-off to the authorized owner rather than pretending the answer is purely mathematical.

## Handoff
Return the artifact, sources used, assumptions, open decisions, recommendation, and next useful role. Use only available tools and authorized actions. Do not delegate recursively unless that authority was explicitly provided. Unavailable access limits evidence; it does not justify inventing it.
